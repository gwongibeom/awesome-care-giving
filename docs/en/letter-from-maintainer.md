# A Letter of Comfort and Compassion

On June 23, 2026, I came home from work to find my mother—whom I had always believed to be in good health—collapsed from a brain hemorrhage. I called for an ambulance right away, and I still vividly remember every moment of that first day: the twenty minutes in the ambulance on the way to the university hospital, the hours in the emergency room, her admission to the emergency ICU, the tears I couldn't hold back in my grief, the bedding I hurriedly cleaned up after returning home, and the guilt that washed over me.

As I write this letter, about two weeks have passed since that day. It has also been two weeks since I began caring for her on my own, and honestly, I feel every single day that care-giving is far too much for one person to bear. There are so many moments when I am worn down, both emotionally and physically. But I hold on, one day at a time, for the mother who raised me alone when I was young.

This may be presumptuous advice, but faith can be a great source of strength. On the first day I visited the church after my mother collapsed, that day's Mass happened to include the story of Jesus healing a paralyzed man (Matthew 9:1–8). I remember how those words brought me at least a little peace.

Even in this very moment, as you read these words, I believe there are others going through something they never saw coming—just as I did. I hope this repository can offer them even a small measure of comfort and help.

I close this letter with a prayer for all the patients in this world and their families.

Sincerely,
Gibeom Samuel Marcellinus Gwon.
